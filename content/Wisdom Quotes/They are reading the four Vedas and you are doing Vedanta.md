Subbaramayya replied, “They are reading the fourth chapter on “Jnana Yoga”. At the time I sat there, it was stated that the karma that a person does after attaining jnana is all Brahmakarma. Everything is Yagna. To illustrate that pomt, the slokas from 25 to the 30 of chapter IV of the Gita were read and it was explained how the various sacrifices done by yogis merge into Brahman. The word ‘/uhvatf was used to indicate that everything is consumed by the sacrificial fire so as to merge ulumately in Brahman. The commentator said that ‘Shrotadeen indriyanyanye samyamagnishu juhvat’ meant that they are controlling the imdriyas (senses). I was not able to understand it properly. Bhagavan may be pleased to explain it clearly.”

Bhagavan, who was tll then reclining, sat up and said:

Others offer as sacrifice their senses of hearing etc. into

the fires of self-control. Other yogis again offer sound and

other objects of perception into the fires of the senses. Gita, IV: 26
