## Who Was Bill Watterson?

Bill Watterson (b. 1958) is an American cartoonist and the creator of *Calvin and Hobbes* (1985-1995), widely regarded as one of the greatest comic strips ever made. Through the adventures of a six-year-old boy and his stuffed tiger -- who is entirely real when no adults are watching -- Watterson explored questions that most philosophy departments only circle around: the nature of reality, the meaning of art, the weight of conformity, and the bottomless depth of a child's wonder.

He famously refused all merchandising to protect the strip's integrity and lives a deliberately private life. In an age of relentless self-promotion, his silence speaks as loudly as his art.

## Core Teaching

Watterson's wisdom is not systematic but atmospheric. Calvin and Hobbes teaches by immersion: in snow-covered woods, in wagon rides down impossible hills, in conversations between a boy and a tiger about the stars. The strip's deepest teaching is that wonder is not a childish phase to outgrow but the natural state of an attentive mind. Imagination is not escape -- it is a more honest engagement with reality than the dry routines adults mistake for maturity.

His refusal to license the strip is itself a teaching: some things lose their meaning the moment you try to sell them.

## Where to Start

Start with [[Calvin and Hobbes]] and let the strips work on you slowly. The philosophical depth reveals itself between the punchlines.
