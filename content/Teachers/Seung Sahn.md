> *"Only don't know."*

## Who Was Seung Sahn?

Seung Sahn (1927-2004) was a Korean Zen master and the first Korean to teach Zen widely in the West. After receiving dharma transmission in the Jogye Order, Korea's largest Buddhist lineage, he traveled to the United States in 1972 with almost no English and no money. He worked in a laundromat in Providence, Rhode Island, and began teaching Zen to anyone who walked through the door.

From these humble beginnings, he founded the Kwan Um School of Zen, now active in over thirty countries. Known for his sharp, humorous teaching style, he combined the rigor of traditional Korean Zen with a warmth and accessibility that broke through cultural barriers. His exchanges with students are legendary -- playful, fierce, and always pointing back to the moment before thinking.

## Core Teaching

Seung Sahn's teaching centers on **"don't-know mind"** -- the practice of holding the great question ("What am I?") without grasping for an answer. When a student offered a clever response, he would often say simply: "That's your opinion. Put it down." He taught that all opinions, all conceptual knowledge, all certainty about the nature of reality must be released before clear seeing can arise.

His method was deceptively simple: sit, breathe, and when a question arises, keep "don't know." Not the paralysis of ignorance, but the aliveness of genuine inquiry. From this open, questioning mind, compassionate action naturally emerges.
