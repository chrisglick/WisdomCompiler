> *"Your own Self-realization is the greatest service you can render the world."*

## Who Was Ramana Maharshi?

Ramana Maharshi (1879-1950) was an Indian sage who attained spontaneous self-realization at the age of sixteen in Madurai, without any teacher, practice, or prior spiritual knowledge. A sudden fear of death overwhelmed him, and in the investigation of "Who dies?" he discovered the deathless Self — an awakening from which he never returned.

He left home immediately for the holy mountain Arunachala in Tiruvannamalai, South India, where he remained for the rest of his life. For years he sat in silent samadhi, barely eating, covered in insect bites, absorbed in the Self. Gradually seekers gathered, and an ashram formed around him — Sri Ramanasramam, which continues to this day.

## Core Teaching

Ramana's teaching is radical simplicity: **Self-enquiry** (*Atma Vichara*). When any thought arises, trace it back to its source by asking "Who am I?" or "To whom does this thought arise?" This is not an intellectual exercise but a turning of attention inward to the source of the I-sense itself. When that source is found, the ego dissolves and what remains is pure consciousness — the Self.

He also taught that **surrender** (*prapatti*) and **self-enquiry** lead to the same goal: "Self-surrender is the same as Self-knowledge."

## Tradition & Lineage

Though he had no formal guru, Ramana is recognized within Advaita Vedanta as one of its greatest modern exponents. His teaching echoes Shankaracharya, the Ribhu Gita, and the Upanishads. He is considered by many to be a direct manifestation of Arunachala (Shiva) itself.
